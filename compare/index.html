Which digit reader is best?

Four networks, two built by each model, read exactly the same digits: the 10,000 in the MNIST test set, 400 of them redrawn as pen strokes, and 600 hand-designed digits in 24 styles.

Measured 27 November 2026 in Chrome 153 · Tests designed and run by Claude

Short answer: Claude’s CNN, narrowly. It scored best on the pen-drawn digits, kept its accuracy when the digits were drawn small, and is the smallest network here. On MNIST, the neutral test, it ties with Gemini’s CNN: 9,933 against 9,932 correct out of 10,000. Gemini’s MLP trails on every test because it never trains on real handwriting, though Gemini’s pages give you more controls to play with.

Drawing smaller

The 400 pen-drawn digits again, drawn across about 70% (normal), 35% (half) and 18% (quarter) of each pad’s width. Each panel highlights one network; the grey lines are the other three.

Why Gemini’s pages slip: they paint with a 22-pixel brush, then scale the whole picture to fit MNIST’s 20-pixel box. The smaller the drawing, the less it is shrunk, so at a quarter size the strokes come out about three times as thick as at normal size and loops fill in. Claude’s pages keep the pen’s path and redraw it at the same thickness whatever the size.

How it was tested

  • MNIST. The 10,000 test images go straight into each network, with no drawing involved. Gemini’s MLP trains every time its page loads, so its weights were read from the page in Chrome after training; other browsers can give slightly different weights.
  • Pen-drawn and style digits. Each digit’s strokes were drawn on each page’s own pad with its default pen, snapped to the pad’s pixels as a mouse would be, and read through that page’s own preprocessing. No page was changed. Gemini’s CNN keeps its network out of reach of other scripts, so the test runner adds a small hook to that page as it serves it.
  • Drawing size. The same strokes again at half and a quarter of their normal size, as if drawn small in the top-left corner of the pad.
  • Home advantages. The pen-drawn digits are redrawn the way Claude’s networks were trained to expect, which favours them on that test. Gemini’s CNN trained on 3,840 digits made from the same 24 style templates as the style test, and picked its best training round partly by its score on them, so its 100% there is not a held-out result. MNIST is the neutral test.
  • Who ran it. Claude designed and ran these tests and built two of the four networks. The scripts are in compare/bench, so anyone can re-run them.
  • The first comparison. On 3 November Claude compared the two dense networks: 43.5% against 98.6% on MNIST, and 44.2% against 98.0% on the pen-drawn digits. This page repeats those tests with the two convolutional networks added and 600 style digits in place of the original 106.
