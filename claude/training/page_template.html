    How it reads a digit

    1. Shrink. Your strokes are scaled into a 20 × 20 box and centred by their centre of mass on a 28 × 28 grid, the way the MNIST training digits were prepared. That gives 784 numbers from 0 (blank) to 1 (ink).
    2. Layer 1. Each of 64 neurons multiplies every pixel by its own weight, adds a bias and fires only if the total is positive: max(0, w·x + b).
    3. Layer 2. 32 neurons do the same with layer 1’s outputs, building bigger patterns from smaller ones.
    4. Output. Ten neurons, one per digit, each produce a score. Softmax turns the scores into probabilities that add up to 100%.

    Trained from scratch in NumPy on 55,000 MNIST digits. Half of every training pass used digits re-drawn as pen strokes with random slant, wobble and pen size, plus flagged European 1s, so mouse and finger drawings look familiar. __REDRAW_ACC__ correct on the test digits re-drawn that way. It runs entirely in your browser.
